But let's start at the beginning... What even is the Pomerania Region? Pomerania is vague area of northern Poland. Some 2/3 of the northern Polish border is aquatic, and the name reflects that (Po(st)-Mer = „after the sea”). It's officially split into 3 warlordships (states/Landen/prefectures/however-you-call-it; And yes: they really are called warlordships in Polish. We tried to culture-wash for internationals by making the official translation sound phonetically like their Polish name „województwo” (which also conveniently happens to mask the name's brutal origins), so you won't see Pomeranian Warlordship in any international shipping details, but us Poles will still know (and I, as a Pole, decided to let you know, too - totally not because I can't spell the official translation and would rather type this for 3min than spend 3s looking it up). Although, obviously, the warlord (wojewoda) is nowadays a democratically-elected office, and acquiring this title doesn't involve conquering any lands or burning and slaughtering any villages.), but this official division is kinda BS. We have the Pomeranian Warlordship, the Western Pomeranian Warlordship and - for some reason beyond my understanding - Kujawy-Pomeranian Warlordship. Kujawy Region is a cultural region of central-northern (on the north-south axis; just central on the east-west) Poland, and it lines up pretty nicely with the Kujawy-Pomeranian Warlordship. Which does NOT touch the sea in any way. So why isn't that warlordship just named after Kujawy Region, and instead they stuck Pomerania in there, is beyond me. The whole point of the name Pomerania is to describe something next to the sea; it's a geographical, not a cultural, term. With that, I'd honestly argue that some 60% of Pomeranian Warlordship (which, btw, is strictly what I mean any time I mention the Pomerania Region (capital P, capital R - like in the section title), in this article or anywhere else, unless otherwise stated) isn't even Pomerania anymore. Which (that absence), in itself, would be fine! I am, generally, a federalist. I acknowledge that a central governing authority isn't always needed. And while a federation is usually defined as a union of regions (countries, counties, anything), a federation of organizations (that themselves are owned/cooperate with the regions) also works perfectly fine. The problem is that - while I acknowledge that a central governing authority isn't always needed - this requires a lot of discipline, trust, and cooperation. Some agreed-upon leading entity (it doesn't have to hold any „official” power; all others just have to trust it and listen to it - and optionally change it out for a new leader (by just... trusting and listening to the old one less), if they end up broadly discontent), for the sake of efficient coordination, issuing new standards, or conflict arbitrage, especially in looser federations, is usually good. The problem with Tricity, is that all these organizations, all with various levels of legal power and legitimacy, are VERY MUCH NOT cooperating well. As I said: some of them claim to either outright be Tricity, or try to establish themselves as the de-facto metropolitan authority of Tricity. This isn't cooperation, it's a Game of Thrones. And for the other group, those that claim to be [something] for/of Tricity, they often end up stepping on each other's toes, in terms of their competence.
