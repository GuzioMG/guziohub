cat /articles/3.txt This will be a comparatively short article, I just need to rant a tiny bit... So, you may have seen this site long-ago-enough to remember that once upon a time there existed a service called GuzioHub Drive. That service was powered by the most overrated piece of self-hosted software on the planet, Nextcloud. Nextcloud is written in PHP. PHP, just like GuzioHub Drive... Has recently fucking died. The "death" of PHP I'm referring to is the widely-publicized (so widely, in fact, that I don't feel inclined to link to any sources because you probably already know it - I'm sure someone in 20yrs, if this blog (or even the internet itself, for that matter) survives that long, will be incredibly displeased by this decision) RCE security vulnerability in PHP. Of course, PHP didn't completely disappear as the result of that incident. It was discovered, patched, and after some very hectic 24h - things mostly came back to normal. Still, basically every PHP site on earth was offline for several hours, so a part of the internet DID die. It simply came back. You know what didn't come back, tho? GuzioHub Drive. This one stays dead. Forever. But why? Nextcloud SUCKS. I hated it from the very 1st day I deployed it, and it was the very first service on GuzioHub. 2 main reasons why it sucks: * It's TOO DAMN HEAVY. The amount of stuff a typical Nextcloud instance does it TRULY UNSETTLING. And... I get it! It's enterprise software. It needs to have many features. If you're an enterprise customer, you'd probably be happy. You know who isn't an enterprise customer? A single fkin user. Even if said user has prospects of expansion to their family and friends... Even on that scale - Nextcloud does way too fkin much. If you're setting up a family cloud for you and your partner - you don't need it. If you're setting up a "friend-cloud" - even if you anticipate expanding to as much as 100 users (say, your friends will be allowed to invite their friends, and so on) - you STILL DON'T NEED IT, trust me. I've ran across many larger-scale friend-clouds since I joined Matrix, and not a single one of those hosts Nextcloud. That's gotta be telling something. What it's telling, is that unless you're a real enterprise - with Debora from the reception desk who hadn't touched a computer in 30 years, and needs an enterprise-certified idiot-proofing level; with 500+ employees; with an IT stack that's already a legacy tangled mess and an extra PHP service slapped onto it will seem right at home; with some enterprise SSO solution - you DO NOT. NEED. NEXTCLOUD. You're gonna get a much better mileage out of your server by just picking individual solutions to problems you actually have. In „Oh, but the integration!” SHUT UP! You want real integration? Set up an SSO gate like Authentik or LLDAP or LLDAP+Authelia (I went from option 2 (worked fine for NextCloud and Matrix/Tuwunel) to 3 when I migrated to Immich, because it didn't support "raw" LDAP and instead I needed to deploy Authelia, as an OAuth2 engine sitting on top of LDAP). I'm an integration nerd, I know what I'm talking about! Integration in NextCloud is a lie. Most actual Apps come with a little "It's only the frontend, please deploy the backend yourself and point to it from the admin settings menu!". Congrats! You managed to add a button to the top-bar with, that opens an iframe! Truly revolutionary tech; I totally couldn't replicate it on this very website if I wanted to, with a dozen of hand-written HTML lines. When I first got interested in NextCloud, I got truly hooked on it because I thought I'm gonna be able to automate the setup of anything else I may want to host, from a site like this to a Minecraft server, via that apps menu. And that it's gonna all be magically integrated, with a single account system and god-knows-what-else. That wasn't the case back then. NextCloud is written in PHP and any apps were also limited to PHP, which excludes a huge portion of services one may want to run because it's a dying language and no New And Cool stuff is written in it anymore (even if we exclude the recent security nightmare, it's not a secret that PHP was bleeding users for a while). NextCloud foundation finally noticed this and allowed Docker containers to be managed by NextCloud, but it's too little too late....... WARNING: THIS ARTICLE IS INCOMPLETE AS OF YET!!!
